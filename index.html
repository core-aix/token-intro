What is a token?

When you type a question into an AI assistant, your words are chopped into small pieces called tokens. The AI reads tokens, writes tokens, and is billed by the token.

Tap one of the real questions below to see how many tokens it takes — and what the answer actually costs.

First, a token is not a word

A token is a chunk of text. Common short words are usually one token each. Longer or more unusual words get split into several pieces. Even the space before a word travels with it.

A short sentence becomes eight tokens:

Notice that Sheffield at the start of the sentence is split into She and ffield, while Library next to it survives as one piece. Odder still: put a space in front and  Sheffield becomes a single token again. Nobody designed these rules — they were learnt from patterns in an enormous amount of text.

📏 As a rough rule of thumb: 1 token ≈ 4 characters ≈ ¾ of an English word. A page of writing is roughly 500 tokens.

So… is that expensive?

On its own, no. Every answer on this page cost less than half a penny. The interesting part is what happens when you multiply.

    This is why the businesses building these tools watch token counts so closely. One question is nothing. A million questions a day is a budget.

    About these numbers

    • Every question on this page was really sent to Claude Haiku 4.5, and every answer shown is the genuine reply, unedited.
    • Costs use that model's published rates: $1.00 per million tokens read and $5.00 per million tokens written. Pounds are converted at approximately £1 = $1.27.
    • Tokens are counted with a widely used open tokeniser. Every AI model chops text up slightly differently; on the examples here this count came within about 3% of the model's own. The idea is identical, the totals shift a little.
    • Models also produce hidden thinking tokens before answering, which are charged as well. The figures here count only the words you can actually see, so a real bill would be somewhat higher.
    • Prices and models change quickly. These figures were prepared in 2026.
